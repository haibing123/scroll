You need somebody, who can build off-the-wall, unusual interactive experiences

Honestly, building unexpected, hands-on experiences is kind of my thing. One of my favorite projects was with the World Food Programme — I spent months in Mozambique designing an AR-based simulation for school meal monitors. The idea was to make training feel like real life: they'd "walk" through a virtual kitchen, spot safety risks, and practice responding under pressure. It was messy, experimental, and so much more engaging than a boring slideshow. I've done similar things in other projects too — like transforming a sensitive consent education course into a comic-book-style narrative with interactive decision points. My goal is always the same: make people feel something while they learn.

You need somebody, who can prototype novel interaction modes and creative applications of AI

I love the scrappy, creative side of prototyping — testing weird ideas and seeing how people respond. At WFP, I helped design a data-driven decision-making toolkit where leaders could experiment with different choices and instantly see how they'd impact operations. I've also prototyped chat-based simulations that mimic real-life conversations, giving people space to "practice" tough decisions before they face them in the field. A lot of my work blends storytelling with experimentation — whether that's a branching scenario that changes based on a user's empathy choices or adaptive learning paths that feel personal and responsive.

You need somebody, who can collaborate with research teams to translate complex ideas into tangible demos

I've spent most of my career translating wildly complex ideas into something humans can actually use. For instance, I worked with legal experts and human rights researchers to turn dense policy texts into practical, scenario-driven courses for judges and police officers across Central Asia. Another time, I partnered with tech and data scientists to build learning experiences around digital transformation and AI adoption — basically turning white papers into walk-through, decision-making experiences that people could explore at their own pace. It's one of my favorite parts of the job: that bridge between the deep technical stuff and the lightbulb moment for the learner.

You need somebody, who can identify high-impact storytelling opportunities and create experiences around them

Storytelling is at the core of everything I design. Whether I'm crafting comic-based consent education, designing crisis simulations for humanitarian responders, or building courses that teach child online protection, I'm always looking for the human story beneath the content. For one project, I built a learning journey around a fictional refugee camp — learners had to make tough choices with limited resources, mirroring real-world constraints. It was part game, part training, and part emotional storytelling — and it stuck with people far more than a traditional course ever would.

You need somebody, who can analyze experimental deployments to generate insights

Every project I run is a living experiment. After we launch, I dive deep into the data — everything from where people click and pause to how long they spend in each section — and then I use that to iterate, tweak, and evolve. At Practicum, for example, we completely overhauled our onboarding journey after analyzing how learners were dropping off in the first few weeks. At UNICEF, I ran multiple rounds of learner testing for a global course on obesity prevention, refining everything from tone to interaction based on real behavior. I see iteration not as the "end" of a project but as where the real creative work starts.
